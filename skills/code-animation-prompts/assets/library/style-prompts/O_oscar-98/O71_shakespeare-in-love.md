## Style prompt words

Elizabethan court miniature painting, learned from Nicholas Hilliard: flat opaque watercolor on warm parchment, extremely fine lines on the dark side (line width is about 1/1000 of the width of the picture), lead white dots on the light-receiving edges, and reddish-brown outlines. Color matching: ultramarine base #203E92, shell gold #D6B05C (fine gold dots with a few polished highlights), carmine #A8202C, lead white #F6F2E8, patina #346C40. Signature image: The subject is painted in a gold-rimmed oval pendant, which is placed flat on a rust-red brocade; a clump of white-flowered wild roses covers the subject's back and surroundings, with a short golden Roman capital inscription on the upper edge. The background color of the oval is always ultramarine, and the landscape and sky are painted as flat ribbons when placed in it. For large scenes, you can leave the pendant and the painting method remains unchanged. The camera pans slowly and slightly, only the flowers, fabrics and gestures are moving. Compare Hilliard's "Youth among the Roses" (c. 1587). Avoid: airbrush gradients; thick oil paint strokes; flat yellow gold.

English:

Elizabethan portrait miniature in Nicholas Hilliard's manner: opaque watercolour laid flat on warm vellum, very fine hatching in shadows (lines about 1/1000 of frame width), lead-white dots on lit edges, red-brown outlines. Palette: ultramarine ground #203E92, shell gold #D6B05C (granular flecks, a few burnished highlights), carmine #A8202C, lead white #F6F2E8, verdigris #346C40. Signature shot: the picture inside a gold-rimmed oval locket lying on rust-red damask; a flowering white eglantine briar fills the oval behind and around the subject; a short motto in gold Roman capitals arcs along the top. The oval's ground stays ultramarine; landscape and sky go in as flat colour bands. Wide scenes may leave the locket, same method, true perspective. Slow push, slight tilt; only flowers, cloth and gestures move. Reference: Hilliard, *Young Man Among Roses* (c. 1587). Avoid: airbrush gradients; thick oil strokes; flat yellow gold.

### Director’s Note

Clawd plays Shakespeare: starched ruff, gold earrings, quill in miniature, white rose on stage. The four shots cut hard, from the private miniature painting to the public theater. In the last shot, the Queen slowly nodded and closed this section.

### Storyboards in the finished film

1. Close shot: A miniature painting of a gold pendant on dark red brocade, Clawd pretending to be Shakespeare, standing among white briars, wearing a wheel-shaped ruff and writing with a quill; the camera slowly zooms in and pans down slightly. (0.8s)
2. Panorama: Rose Theater. The camera pans down from the painted marquee and stands above and zooms in to the stage, where Clawd stands holding a white rose and the Queen sits in the right box. (0.6s)
3. Medium shot: Balcony scene, Juliet is holding her chin from above, and Clawd is holding a white rose to her from below; the camera is slightly zoomed out. (0.6s)
4. Close shot: Queen Elizabeth in the box under the canopy, turns away from watching the show, shakes her fan, and nods slowly; the camera zooms in slightly. (0.6s)
