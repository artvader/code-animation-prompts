## Style prompt words

The high-profile black-and-white location photography of the early 1950s uses 2D painting to create a photo-like feel: no line drawing or flat painting, only soft gradients and delicate textures (sand grains, rock pits, cloth grains). Light and shade: About 70% of the picture is brighter than medium gray; the sky is light to medium gray, only one stop darker than the bright clouds; there are very few deep blacks. The default is the hard sun at noon, with short and clear shadows; the sky remains light and bright structures remain in other lights. Water, spray or fog washes away the brightest spots, leaving lace details; the wet surface reflects the sky. Finishing as printed: soft S-curve, slightly soft lens, high halo, fine grain refreshed every frame (±4%), light vignetting, slight flickering. Only the main character around whom the story revolves retains color (such as #D97757), and the light and shade also have a photo feel. Lens: Pan down from the sky, look straight into the frame, and then slowly zoom out. Benchmarking photographer Burnett Guffey ("Loyal in Troubled Times"). Avoid: low-key and dull, dead white, cartoonish flat painting.

English:

Early-1950s high-key black-and-white photography, painted in 2D to read as a photo: no outlines or flat fills, only soft gradients plus fine texture (sand, rock pitting, cloth). Values: about 70% of the frame above mid-grey; sky light-mid grey, one step below the bright clouds; deep blacks small. Default: hard midday sun, short crisp shadows; under other light keep the pale sky and bright values. Water, foam and mist blow out at the peaks, keeping lace detail; wet surfaces mirror the sky. Print finish: gentle S-curve, slightly soft lens, halation, fresh fine grain (±4%), light vignette, flicker. Only the hero the story centres on keeps colour, whole figure (e.g. #D97757), shaded photographically. Camera: tilt down from the sky, tight level frame, slow pull-back. Reference: cinematographer Burnett Guffey (*From Here to Eternity*). Avoid: low-key frames, white blanks, cartoon fills.

### Director’s Note

Clawd plays Pruitt: olive boat hat, holding the bugle from the poster. A shot of the sky pans down into the bay, and a shot of waves in the middle covers the kiss. When it cuts to a panoramic shot, Clawd steps on the beat and raises the bugle to his mouth, as if blowing the trumpet for this scene.

### Storyboards in the finished film

1. Driving over Halona Bay, a group of planes flew through the cumulus clouds; the camera panned down to the sea. (0.5s)
2. Looking straight at the lovers in the tightly framed waves: The waves rush up to cover them, and then recede into lace-like foam. The two keep kissing; the camera slows down and drifts to the right. (1.1s)
3. Panoramic view of the bay: Clawd, wearing a boat-shaped hat, sits on a lava stone platform, raising a bugle and blowing to his lover; the camera zooms out slightly. (0.9s)
