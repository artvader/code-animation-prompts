## Style prompt words

Each frame is treated as a photojournalistic spread from a 1946 pictorial. Start by rendering the scene as a photograph in gray tones (either 3D or hand-drawn): low angle sun, long shadows, deep focus, close up as clear as the horizon. The photo spans two full pages, with a vertical center crease running through the middle and a slight shadow. Then printed with 45° black dots, spacing approximately 1/250 of the frame width, off-white coated paper #ECE6D8, ink #1A1A1A, slightly expanded. From the first shot onwards, a small black patch of title and captions is overprinted in a quiet area. Only the protagonist around whom the story revolves should be in a solid orange version (such as #D97757), change the screen angle, and be wrong by 2–3 pixels, leaving only the darkest part of the black version; the protagonist should occupy at least a quarter of the height of the screen. Lens: Go into the panoramic view, advance a detail, zoom out again, and the photo shrinks into an open magazine. Comparing photographer Gregg Toland, Life magazine's postwar photography feature. Avoid: The center seam cuts through the main character, and the dots are so thick that the photo breaks.

---

English:

Each frame is a 1946 picture-magazine photo spread. Render the scene as a grey photo (3D or painted): low sun, long shadows, deep focus (near and far both sharp). The photo bleeds across both pages, crossed by a soft vertical gutter crease. Print as a 45° black halftone, dot pitch about 1/250 of frame width, ink #1A1A1A on off-white coated stock #ECE6D8. A short black headline and caption sit in a quiet area. Only the hero the story centres on, at least 1/4 of frame height, prints on its own spot-orange plate (e.g. #D97757) at another angle, 2-3 px off, black only in its deepest shadows. Camera: walk in, push to a detail, pull back to reveal an open magazine. References: cinematographer Gregg Toland; *Life* post-war photo essays. Avoid: the gutter cutting the hero, coarse dots that break the photo.

### Director’s Note

Clawd plays Fred: the captain's boat hat and the woolen collar of his B-3 flight jacket. It walked along the corridor of the bomber, stopped and raised its head while stepping on the beat; cut to the nose cabin where it was looking, and then zoomed out all the way, and it turned out that the entire scene was a page from a magazine in 1946. Two hard cuts, the last shot is a continuous zoom out.

### Storyboards in the finished film

1. Deep focus panorama from a low position, aircraft graveyard: Clawd, wearing a boat hat and a vertical wool collar, walks under the wing of a stripped B-17, stops, looks up at the glass nose; the camera moves to the right and zooms in. (0.9s)
2. Looking out from the bomber's glass nose cabin, rows of bombers stretch towards the distant mountains, with Clawd at the left edge of the screen; the camera slows down. (0.8s)
3. Zoom out: The photo is indented into an open magazine, titled GRAVEYARD OF THE FORTRESSES, placed on a dark tabletop. (0.8s)
