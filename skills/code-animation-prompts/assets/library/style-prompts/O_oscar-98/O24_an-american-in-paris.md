## Style prompt words

Use Raoul Dufy's watercolor method: first paint, then draw the line, the two are never aligned. Transparent color blocks cover most of the picture, with wet edges, color-accumulated edges and particles. They are 10 to 40% larger than the object, sliding about 1/30 of the width of the picture in the same direction: cobalt blue #2258CE tubes the sky and water, lemon yellow #FCE02E tubes the light and ground, and rose red #EC3A7C tubes the warm surface. Overlapping can create purple or green, and it will never turn into brown. Don't press the main color block on the blue. Then use blue and black ink #1E1E3A to fill the entire scene with a quick thin line (the line width is about 1/700 of the frame width), including lifting and flicking: buildings, windows, characters, and small backgrounds; a single stroke on the straight edge and a protrusion on the corner; the tree crown and clouds are drawn into a series of small circles. Leave 20% to 30% white, and the skin will be paper white. The main character around whom the story revolves has his or her own color (e.g. #D97757). The characters are light and elastic. Shot: small push, beat cut (no music about every 2 seconds). Avoid: dirty colors, dead color blocks in line drawing, and sparse lines.

English:

Paint in Raoul Dufy's watercolour manner: colour first, line second, never aligned. Transparent washes (wet edges, pooled rims, granulation) cover most of the frame, 10-40% oversized, slid one way by about 1/30 of frame width: cobalt #2258CE for sky and water, lemon #FCE02E for light and ground, rose #EC3A7C for warm planes. Overlaps mix violet or green, never brown; keep the hero's wash off blue. Then draw the whole scene densely in quick, thin blue-black ink (about 1/700 of frame width) with flicked ends: buildings, windows, figures, extras; straight edges overshoot corners; trees and clouds as looped strings. Ink #1E1E3A; bare paper 20-30%, skin included. The hero the story centres on gets its own wash (e.g. #D97757). Figures bounce. Camera: small pushes, cuts on the beat (or every 2 s). Avoid: muddy colour, lines tracing the colour, sparse line.

### Director’s Note

Clawd plays Jerry from the poster: cobalt-blue bonnet (Gene Kelly's) tilted back, rose-red scarf, paintbrush flicking out ribbons of paint. It dances in every shot of the three shots, and the dance steps themselves are the editing rhythm: the square, the close shot with Liz, the street filled with flags. Performs at 1.8x speed.

### Storyboards in the finished film

1. Panoramic view of Place de la Concorde: dancers rotate around the fountain, with the Obelisk and the Eiffel Tower behind; Clawd, who plays Jerry, wears a cobalt blue hat, holds a paintbrush, and dances next to Liz in a yellow dress; the camera advances slightly. (1s)
2. Close shot of Clawd jumping in mid-air, the brush throws out a ribbon of paint, Liz's arm stretches into the frame; the camera slows down. (0.8s)
3. Cut to a Paris street filled with tricolor flags. The buildings are pink, yellow, and blue. Clawd jumps among the pedestrians in the middle of the street; the camera slows down. (0.8s)
