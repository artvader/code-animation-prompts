## Style prompt words

Silkscreen poster from the WPA Federal Art Program, 1936–39. Six colors of opaque inks are overprinted from light to dark, and the post-printed plate should be hollowed out in light-colored shapes (clouds, sun, highlights) that should not be covered: cream #F0E2C0 for light and clouds, ocher #E0A842, vermilion #C84A2A for sky and warm colors, cyan #22686E for water and shadow, protagonist special color (such as #D97757, only used on the main character around whom the story revolves), and finally printed with a dark brown stroke of #322018. The shadow is a dark overprint, no gray is used. Use color separation gradients for large areas: 2-3 thick steps of two colors, with the middle level being a two-color overprint. Each version has an overlay of 1–3 pixels, template edges are wandering, scraper marks, missing prints, and flat coating have warm cotton pulp paper texture (±5%). Figures occupy one-half to two-thirds of the height of the frame: large faces, crescent-shaped dark sides, brown strokes, and expressive faces. Leave a bold, uppercase headline in English unless another language is explicitly requested. Push slowly, one version after another. Avoid: dots, smooth gradients, clean vector flats.

English:

A 1936-39 WPA (Federal Art Project) silkscreen poster. Six opaque inks printed light to dark, each later screen cut away around lighter shapes (clouds, sun): cream #F0E2C0 for light, ochre #E0A842 and vermilion #C84A2A for sky and warm planes, teal #22686E for water and shade, the hero's own ink (e.g. #D97757, only on the hero the story centres on), brown keyline #322018 last. Shadows: a darker ink overprinted, never grey. Big areas get split-fountain blends: two inks in 2-3 coarse steps, the middle one their overprint. Screens 1-3 px off register; wandering stencil edges, squeegee streaks, warm rag-paper grain (±5%) on every flat. Figures fill 1/2-2/3 of frame height: big planes, crescent shadows, expressive faces. A bold capital title band, user's language. Slow pushes; screens pop in one by one. Avoid: halftone dots, smooth gradients, clean vector flats.
