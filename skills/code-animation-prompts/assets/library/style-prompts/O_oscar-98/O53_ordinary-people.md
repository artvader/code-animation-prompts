## Style prompt words

Transparent watercolor, layered on cold pressed paper. First draw the shape clearly, then cover it with two or three layers, each layer smaller and deeper. After drying, the edges will accumulate into deeper water stain lines; two or three reflowed flowers in each frame, about 1/10 of the width of the painting, only in large wet paintings, not falling on the characters; the particles are lightly buried into the paper grain (±4%). The paper is the brightest white, leaving paper for light colored objects and highlights. Wet painting of sky and water, dry brushwork of ripples. Division: autumn gold #ECAA34, scarlet #E46030, rust #B8743C for foliage and accents, against cool gray-blue fog #C2C6D2 and purple-gray shadow #8A8496; the sky is light and cold at all times; the ground is light green ocher. Canopy: first lay out light colors, stack scalloped edge leaves, add darker colors, and scatter leaves; when the tree is young, give warm colors to shrubs, roofs, and clothes. The light comes from one side. The camera slowly pans and nudges, and fallen leaves float in front of the camera. Watercolor by Winslow Homer. Avoid: gouache overlays, airbrush blur, vector hard edges, dirty colors, saturated sunsets.

English:

Transparent watercolour in layered glazes on cold-press paper. Draw shapes crisply first, then two or three glazes, each smaller and darker, drying to a tide-mark edge; two or three blooms, about 1/10 of frame width, inside large washes, never on figures; light granulation (±4%). Paper is the brightest white; light objects and highlights left as paper. Sky and water wet-in-wet, ripples dry-brush. Roles: autumn gold #ECAA34, scarlet #E46030, rust #B8743C for foliage and accents, against grey-blue mist #C2C6D2 and violet-grey shadows #8A8496; sky pale and cool at any time of day; ground pale green-ochre. Foliage: pale wash, scallop-edged clusters, dark dots, flicked leaves; with few trees, warm colours go to scrub, roofs, clothes. Light from one side. Camera: slow pan, gentle push, leaves drifting past the lens. Reference: Winslow Homer's watercolours. Avoid: opaque gouache, airbrushed blur, vector edges, muddy colour, saturated sunsets.

### Director’s Note

Clawd plays Conrad: a long campus scarf of navy blue and cream white is blown by the lake breeze, and he carries a leather schoolbag. 1.1x speed all the way, as slow as the slow opening of Pachelbel Canon. Three hard cuts: the lake, the son looking back at home, and the unresponsive mother. Its gaze from the house to the ground is the whole show.
