## Style prompt words

1950s UPA modernist cartoons. Ink line #221C19 is about 1/400 of the width of the painting, with thickness and occasional disconnection; the color blocks are staggered in one direction relative to the ink line by about 1/300. The object is made up of overlapping flat pieces: a light-receiving surface, plus a flat dark surface of the same color and about one and a half percent darker. Bright and transparent: the light aqua sky and cream paper #F2E9D3 occupy most of the picture. The mustard yellow #E3A92B, turquoise #2B8C86, and orange red #D4452B are just for embellishment; the sky, grass green, and gray blue are obtained by mixing these colors with the paper color. At other times, only the sky color is changed. Light pastel grain throughout (brightness ±5%), minor paper white flecks. Poster composition: A large figure (a building, a sun disk, or a mountain) next to an angular silhouetted figure. The beam is drawn as a light-colored flat-coated wedge. Hard cuts between camera positions every two seconds, slow parallax within the segment. Compare UPA's "Gerald McBoing-Boing" (1950) and Saul Bass's "Around the World in Eighty Days" ending (1956). Avoid: gradients, too accurate color registration, and saturated colors filling the screen.

English:

1950s UPA modernist cartoon. Uneven ink lines #221C19 about 1/400 of frame width, sometimes broken; colour blocks sit about 1/300 of frame width off them, all one way. Objects are overlapping flat cut-out facets: a lit face and a shadow face one flat step (about 15%) darker. Pale aqua sky and cream paper #F2E9D3 cover most of the frame; mustard #E3A92B, teal #2B8C86, orange-red #D4452B are accents; sky, greens and slate blues are these mixed toward paper. Other times of day change only the sky colour. Crayon grain ±5% brightness everywhere, few flecks. Poster layout: one big graphic shape (a building or sun disc), angular outlined figures beside it. Light beams: flat pale wedges. Hard cuts every 1–2 s; slow parallax drift. After UPA, *Gerald McBoing-Boing* (1950); Saul Bass, *Around the World in 80 Days* titles (1956). Avoid: gradients, perfect registration, saturated colour everywhere.
