## Style prompt words

Hand-colored natural history etching. All objects are drawn with warm black and brown engraved lines: dense parallel lines and cross lines (the line distance is about 1/400 of the width of the picture, and the lines are thicker in the dark), stipples are used for soft transitions, and there are line projections under the objects; the sky is left paper-colored or only a few sparse horizontal lines. Flat watercolor applied within lines, medium saturated, recognizable colors: Paper #EFE3C4, Ochre #5A3A22, Rust Red #B4431E, Yellow #E6AD24, Gray Purple #7D6F8E. Plate: The main drawing 1 is in the center, accounting for about 60% of the height of the page, surrounded by 3-6 numbered small pictures (parts or related objects), each about one-fifth of the height; the upper part is a spaced small capital title, the lower part is a numbered legend, embossed beveled frame, light mold spots. The default is uniform daylight, and the time period changes only to adjust the color to cool and warm; the dark room turns cold, and the side is hard light. Lens: There is only one thing moving (insect, leaf, hand) in the still picture, and then push it slowly and at a constant speed to get to the key details. Compare Moses Harris, "The Aurelian" (1766). Avoid: Smooth gradients; harsh colors; subjects shrinking into small vignettes.

English:

A hand-coloured natural-history copperplate. Every object in engraved warm black-brown line: dense parallel and cross-hatching (spacing about 1/400 of frame width, thicker in darks), stipple for soft tones, hatched shadows beneath; sky bare or sparsely lined. Flat watercolour tints inside the line, medium saturation, clearly coloured: paper #EFE3C4, umber #5A3A22, rust #B4431E, yellow #E6AD24, grey-violet #7D6F8E. Plate: the subject as Fig. 1 in the centre, about 60% of plate height, 3–6 numbered part or object figures around it, each about 1/5 high; spaced small-caps title, numbered legend, bevelled plate mark, light foxing. Even daylight; time of day only shifts the tints' warmth; dark interiors go cold, hard side light. Camera: a still plate where one thing moves (insect, leaf, hand), then a slow push to a key detail. Reference: Moses Harris, *The Aurelian* (1766). Avoid: smooth gradients; garish colour; a tiny vignetted subject.

### Director’s Note

Clawd first appears as "Picture 4" on the board, and then plays Clarice wearing an FBI trainee ID card. The moth falls in the first few shots; the hard cut to the cell and the shot towards Lecter are done in one shot without any cuts in between, and his smile is left until the end. Scene time is approximately 1.8 times screen time.
