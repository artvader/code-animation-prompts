## Style prompt words

Serious crayon drawings on white paper by children aged six to eight. The crayon only rubs until the paper grain is raised; light application reveals 40 to 60% of the paper white, and fills in with force; the lower layer is revealed when overlaying colors. The outline is made in black or dark brown crayon, waxy and shaky, about 1/300 of the width of the painting. The coloring is done back and forth, each stroke is about 1/40 of the width of the painting and about 1/250 of the width. It is painted in rows and punches out the outline. Each piece has its own direction. The spacing and angle are uneven and cannot be arranged in a regular network. Basic colors: red #E0382C, orange #F28A1E, yellow #F7D23A, green #3AA345, blue #2E6BD8. Conventions for children's drawings: ground line, flattened perspective, arrowheads, smiling sun, reverse a letter, and paint patterns one by one. Text is written in English unless another language is explicitly requested, with trembling crayon strokes, without fonts. Lens: First shake the close-up view, and then smoothly scan the entire painting. Avoid: adult-like neat painting techniques, scribbling without details, uniform dot-like or mesh-like textures, and smooth gradients.

English:

A careful 6–8-year-old's wax-crayon drawing on white paper. Crayon catches the paper grain's peaks; light pressure leaves 40–60% paper showing, heavy pressure fills it; overlaps show through. Outlines in black or dark-brown crayon, waxy, wobbling, about 1/300 of frame width. Fills are back-and-forth scribbles, strokes about 1/40 of frame width long and 1/250 wide, rows overshooting the outlines, each area in its own direction, spacing and angle uneven, never a regular mesh. Primary box: red #E0382C, orange #F28A1E, yellow #F7D23A, green #3AA345, blue #2E6BD8. Child conventions: a ground line, flattened perspective, arrowed labels, a smiling sun, one mirrored letter, patterns coloured cell by cell. Text in English unless another language is explicitly requested, in wobbly crayon strokes, not a typeface. Camera: shaky close-ups, then a calm drift. Avoid: neat adult rendering, random scrawl, uniform stipple or mesh, smooth gradients.

### Director’s Note

Clawd plays Ted: wearing a blue apron string and holding a spatula; Billy stands on a chair beating eggs. The first and last times use the same pot for matching editing, and the relationship between father and son is completed in one jump cut.
