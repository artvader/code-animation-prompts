## Style prompt words

Two plates: one is a high-contrast black halftone photo at 45°, with dots about 1/200 of the width of the frame, and almost no midtones; the bottom is a hand-brushed flat color block, staggered about 1/100 of the width of the frame relative to the photo, with crooked edges: taxi yellow #F3BE14, skin powder #F2A197, cream #EFE6CF, red #E2462B, and the bottom is brushed silver paint with a soft luster. #BABCC0. The only protagonist uses the strongest color. The pressure of the squeegee is uneven: in some places, the dots are smeared into ink balls, and in other places, they are dragged into horizontal dry brush stripes and pinholes, and there are vague canvas lines underneath. Repetition is composition: the same character or object is printed twice and three times side by side on an empty silver base, with slight overlap. The scene appears in repetition only as a faded silver print. Lens: hand-held shaking, zooming in, frontal plane freeze. Comparing Warhol's "Double Elvis" (1963). What to avoid: Clean and even dots, perfectly aligned color registration, and crispness everywhere.

English:

Andy Warhol photo-silkscreen. Two plates: a high-contrast black halftone photograph at 45°, dots about 1/200 of frame width, few mid-tones; and hand-painted flat colour blocks underneath, offset about 1/100 of frame width with wobbly edges: taxi yellow #F3BE14, skin pink #F2A197, cream #EFE6CF, red #E2462B, on brushed silver paint #BABCC0 with a soft sheen. The one hero subject takes the strongest block colour. Uneven squeegee pressure: dots flood into blotches in places, drag into dry horizontal streaks and pinholes elsewhere, over faint canvas weave. Repetition is the composition: the same figure or object, full-length, printed side by side two then three times, slightly overlapping, on empty silver. Settings appear only as drained-to-silver prints in the repeat. Camera: handheld shake, a push in, flat frontal tableaux. Reference: Andy Warhol, *Double Elvis* (1963). Avoid: clean even halftone, perfect registration, uniform crispness.

### Director’s Note

Clawd as Joe Buck: black Stetson hat, red scarf, suede fringe on his arms. Let's play out the phrase "I'm walkin' here!" from the movie first: the taxi brakes suddenly, jumps back, pulls the rope and slaps the front of the car and yells. The scene then changes to a Warhol screen, with Clawd posing in Elvis' gunslinger stance, printing first twice, then three times.

### Storyboards in the finished film

1. The crosswalk on Sixth Avenue: The yellow taxi suddenly braked and rushed in. Clawd (Joe Buck) jumped back and stared. He slapped the front of the car with the rope; he shook his hand and slowly pushed it closer. (1.1s)
2. Close shot of Lasso: holding a cigarette and yelling at the taxi, pointing at the driver (I'm walkin' here!); pushing closer. (0.6s)
3. The street scene fades to silver and is printed into Warhol's Double: two Clawds wearing Stetson hats and holding revolvers are stacked together, and then become three; a freeze-frame of a head-on view. (0.8s)
