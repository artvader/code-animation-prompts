## Style prompt words

Lithoprinted poster from the 1920s, four colors: ocean blue #245A8C, ocher yellow #D49640, dark brown #40271B, warm red #E47E62, printed on aged cream paper #F1E7D1, each color offset by approximately 1/700 of the frame width. All tones are composed of ink particles, which are unevenly applied: the particles are about 1/1000 of the width of the picture, and are gathered into soft masses. The light color area covers 20 to 40%, the middle tone covers 50 to 80%, and the solid color is almost full, leaving pinholes; there should be no scattered isolated spots on the blank paper. Division of labor: blue for the sky, water and cool shadows; ocher for the ground and skin; dark brown for outlines, shadows and clothes; a small amount of red. Light and shade are quiet and shallow. The character is classic: the light-receiving side is ocher yellow, the limbs follow a dark brown light and dark line, the head is about one-eighth of the height; many people are arranged in a horizontal line. The movement is slow, relying on water spray, dust, hair, and cloth to bring it out. Lens: Telephoto, front-facing, low to the ground; serifed title banner can be added. A reference to Jean Droit's 1924 Paris Olympics poster. Avoid: coarse noise, pictographic figures, and vector flat paintings.

English:

1920s stone lithograph poster in four inks: sea blue #245A8C, ochre #D49640, deep brown #40271B, warm red #E47E62, on aged cream #F1E7D1, misregistered about 1/700 of frame width. Every tone is ink grain, never a flat fill: specks about 1/1000 of frame width in soft clumps, covering 20–40% of pale areas, 50–80% of mid-tones, nearly all of solids (with pinholes); no stray specks on bare paper. Roles: blue for sky, water, cool shadow; ochre for ground and skin; brown for outlines, darks, clothes; red sparingly. Values calm and pale. Classical figures: ochre lit side, brown core-shadow line down each limb, head 1/8 of body; groups in a row. Slow motion carried by spray, dust, hair, cloth. Camera: long-lens front views, ground-level shots; optional serif title band. Reference: Jean Droit, 1924 Paris Olympics poster. Avoid: coarse speckle, pictogram figures, vector fills.
