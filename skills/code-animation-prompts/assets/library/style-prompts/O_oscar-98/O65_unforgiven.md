## Style prompt words

Frederick Remington Western Oil Painting. The main tone is sunset, which is used before it gets dark: the golden-red sky from orange to deep red is painted with long horizontal strokes, the sun presses on the horizon, the land and characters are dark and warm silhouettes, and there is a thin warm outline light on the sunward side. The second tone is only used after dark: blue-green night scene (sky #17333A, moonlight side #9CC4BC), warm yellow window light #FFCC6A spread on dark ground #1E1C16, plus rain. First draw a clear large shape, and then add brush strokes: the width of each stroke is about 1/80 of the width of the painting, and the length is three to six times the width. The paint is full and the tip of the brush is loose; the difference between the light and shade of each stroke and the background color is only ±6%, and it follows all sides. The canvas pattern should be light. The horizon is low, the sky accounts for 50% to 60%, and the characters are small to medium. Shot: slow panoramic shot from silhouette to character. Comparing Remington's late night scenes and photographer Jack N. Green ("Unforgiven", 1992). Avoid: Smooth gradients; hard-edged ribbons; full-screen brushstrokes that break up shapes; gray characters in warm scenes; large panning.

English:

Frederic Remington-style Western oil painting. Primary register, for anything before full dark: sunset, a red-gold sky (orange into deep red) in long horizontal strokes, the sun on the horizon, land and figures dark warm silhouettes, thin warm rim sunward. Second register, only after dark: a blue-green nocturne (sky #17333A, moonlit edges #9CC4BC, warm window light #FFCC6A on dark ground #1E1C16, rain). Broad, readable shapes first, then loaded strokes about 1/80 of frame width wide, 3–6 times as long, soft broken ends, each within ±6% brightness of the colour beneath, following the surface. Light canvas texture. Low horizon, sky 50–60% of the frame, figures small to medium. Camera: one slow push from a silhouetted wide to the figure. References: Remington's late nocturnes; cinematographer Jack N. Green (*Unforgiven*, 1992). Avoid: smooth gradients; hard-edged flat bands; shattered shapes; grey figures in warm scenes; wide pans.
